### Core Questions:

1. What positions do binary-driven funds already take in listed options?

2. How do they hedge into a catalyst today, and what does it cost them?

3. What do options market makers earn, and what makes them pull back / adjust theos more aggressively?

4. How does liquidity in listed options work?

5. Why are options on catalyst names illiquid?

6. How is adverse selection contained in practice?

7. How large is the hedging flow these funds could bring?'



### Assumptions:

1. Funds with catalyst exposure want protection and can't buy it well today.

2. Listed options are costly or missing at the size and specificity hedgers need.

3. Catalyst outcomes are close to independent, so the capital charge per contract falls as a warehouse's book grows.

4. Identity checks, documentation and contract terms keep adverse selection small enough to price.

5. Hedgers will pay a premium that covers warehouse capital, adverse selection, and the arranger fee.

6. A brokered quote competition with lead-follow syndication prices risk fairly without a standing market.

7. A contract type attracts enough repeat demand to survive.

8. The holders who most want protection are free to buy it when they want it.
