Abs ; *model demand-pressure effects on options prices.*

Model shows that demand pressure in one contract increases its price by some amount proportional to the variance of the unhedgeable part of the option.

If there's some covariance between this and another contract, the price of the other contract increases by an amount proportional to that covariance of the unhedgeable parts of the two options.

Methods ; *identify aggregate positions of dealers and end-users & show that demand-pressure effects are significant*

* dealers trade an arbitrary # of contracts on the same underlying at discrete times
* some risks net out due to correlation between contracts
* dealers can hedge remaining risk by trading underlyings and risk-free bonds
* generalized distribution of the underlyings accomodates some just risk and vol
* END USERS RATIONALE FOR TRADING DOESN'T MATTER





***no-arb theory cannot fully explain options prices***

***opts MMs cannot fully hedge their inventory risk; opts demand therefore influences prices***





BSM theory suggests that all intermediaries (MMs and prop-shops) providing liquidity by taking 'other-side' of demand of the end users (of opts) can hedge perfectly. in that case, opts prices are determined by no-arb and demand pressure doesn't matter.



Why can't intermediaries hedge perfectly?

1. hard to trade continuously

2. stochastic vol

3. underlying equity jumps

4. transaction costs



*so how do competitive risk-averse dealers who CANNOT hedge perfectly* price opts?
